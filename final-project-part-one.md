# Final Project - Part 1

# Tanks and Drones: Evolution on the Battlefield

# Outline 
The modern battlefield is a place of constant change; tactics evolve in response to technological innovation. Since the start of the War in Ukraine, Russia has sent thousands of tanks to the front, only to find out the hard way that they are being beaten by relatively inexpensive drones. In light of this conflict's progression over the past few years, my project poses the question: what does the fight between tanks and drones mean for the future of warfare? My project seeks to answer this question and show how the use of drones in Ukraine has revealed major vulnerabilities in tank protection. Additionally, I will compare the cost to produce a tank vs. a drone, and how the future of the tank is not just a military matter, but also an economic one. 

For simplification, the word "tank" will encompass only Main Battle Tanks (MBTs) and Infantry Fighting Vehicles (IFVs), two of the most common vehicle types Russia has fielded in its war against Ukraine. My analysis will show how many of both types have been destroyed in Ukraine between 2022 and 2025. I will also display the number of drones Ukraine produced/procured during that same time period. With this information, I will then use both data sources to highlight the statistics of an ever-evolving warzone. Although not every tank was destroyed by a drone, the meteoric rise of drone warfare demonstrates a clear shift in strategy, one in which the tank is no longer the centerpiece. 

## Project Outline

1. The Beginning: Tanks were once considered kings of the battlefield...until the drones came.
2. The Target Audience: Policymakers and people interested in military affairs
3. The Main Idea: The introduction of low-cost drones in Ukraine has created a new vulnerability for tanks
4. Why This is Important: This kind of emerging technology could pose a threat to not only tanks, but also other conventional weapons systems in the long term
5. Call to Action: Tanks should incorporate anti-drone technology as a regular feature in its defense systems.

## The Data
The difficult aspect of tracking a war is that the data is not always clean; specifically, although there is open-source data that tracks Russian tank losses (see below), the same cannot be said for Ukrainian drone production. Concerning the latter, even among reputable, nonpartisan sources, figures slightly vary. Given this difficulty, the data I was able to find on Ukrainian drone production/procurement came from university research institutes or nonpartisan think tanks. Ukraine's Ministry of Defence also has production figures for 2024 and 2025, but I wanted to lean more toward politically neutral sources. However, they will be available in the references section at the bottom, since I cross-referenced their information with the sources being used below. 

Warspotting is an open-source database that has been tracking Russian equipment losses in Ukraine since their invasion in February 2022. It allows users to sort through data by vehicle type, whether it was destroyed, damaged, captured, or abandoned, and the given time from (e.g., February 2022 - December 2022). The other four sources outline Ukrainian drone production statistics from 2022 - 2025, which each source correlating with a given year. All of the sources on drones, except for Aviation Week, are university research institutes/ think tanks. Important to note that Aviation Week is still a reputable source in the aerospace field, but operates closer to a news outlet.
