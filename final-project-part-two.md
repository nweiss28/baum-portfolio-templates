# User research 

## Target audience
Initially, I targeted my research toward stakeholders at armored vehicle manufacturers, such as General Dynamics Land Systems (GDLS). My goal was to inform these individuals about drones as an emerging threat against their tanks. However, I realized this approach might not be the most worthwhile because producers like GDLS already know about drones; their M1E3 Abrams prototype includes drone countermeasures (congress.gov 2026). Given this information, along with some of the feedback I was getting from my classmates, I slightly pivoted to target policymakers and analysts who are interested and/or involved in U.S. defense policy. 

## Interview script

| Goal | Questions to Ask |
| Polish my data visualizations; a reader should not have to be technically adept to understand what's being conveyed | Does this data/graph make sense? What stylistic improvement would you suggest? | 
| Visually polish my presentation so that it grabs and keeps my audience's attention | Any color suggestions? Is the presentation aesthetically pleasing? |
| Structure the Shorthand story in a way that is concise but still impactful | Is the story being conveyed in my presentation easy to follow? Is the structure confusing at any point? |

## Interview findings

| Questions | My Interviews | 
| Is there anything confusing about the bar graph? | A MAM student suggested I remove the white space between the bars in my bar graph. For reference, Tableau's side-by-side bar chart spaces categorized bars slightly apart from each other. He suggested I use Datawrapper instead. |
| How does the presentation look? | A fellow MSPPM student suggested that I incorporate brighter colors to help balance out the darker ones already built into Shorthand's template. |
| Do you find the story's structure easy to understand? | Another student said that I could use my recommendations for how tanks could stay relevant in the age of drones as a call-to-action. |
